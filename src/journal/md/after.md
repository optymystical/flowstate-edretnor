## The audience and the wishes
- **How many wishes:** a party on the king's path gets 1. It gets a 2nd for delivering **30+ civilians** to the king before the recall, or for killing the Cell Master. Both can't happen in practice, since killing him means there's no one left to save, so 2 is the maximum.
- **What a wish can be:**
  - The head start on one of hooks 1–7. Only one hook, even with two wishes: the party can only go one way.
  - A material wish: a rare artifact from the royal armory, a sum of money, a couple of acres of open land as a base, or a vessel or vehicle (a ship, a Dwarven automated power cart). Good for a peasant, but modest; nothing as grand as a mansion.
  - Information: the king asks the Royal Guard. If they know, that's the wish; if not, the wish isn't spent.
  - A party can skip the hooks entirely and spend every wish on material ones (the "eighth hook": do whatever they want).
- **Killing the Cell Master always earns an audience**, whatever else happened.
- **The Elven path:** otherwise, the party gets no audience if at least two of these are true:
  1. They didn't take first place in both Round 1 and Round 2.
  2. They delivered **fewer than 10** civilians to the king.
  3. They never reached the king on their own. Being brought in by the Royal Guard like any other civilian doesn't count; coming to him of their own accord to help does.
- **Why 30 and 10** (rough crawl simulation, 4,000 runs; only civilians delivered to the king's cistern before the recall count):

  | Party approach | Median delivered | 30+ | Under 10 |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Takes everyone it meets | 24 | 33% | 3% |
  | Takes most | 17 | 9% | 25% |
  | Mostly ignores them | 0 | 1% | 78% |

## The clock under every hook
The campaign starts around 1639, and the Maw kills the Prophet in 1648: a nine-year slide into the Great Cult Uprising. Three slow threads run under whichever hook the party picks:
- **The Maw's road to 1648.** Edretnor was its first large strike. Every hook can show the cult growing; party action can delay or change the assassination.
- **The Watchers notice them.** They look for people of exceptional potential, the in-world reason PCs stand out. Royal Guard favor, a first-place finish or Blackwell's interest are early signs of a slow recruitment arc.
- **Edretnor won't stay buried.** The Death Herald rises in the ruins a few days later, and the Sympathizer's letter shows someone in Elven government helped plan the attack.

## How the hooks are delivered
- **King's path:** at the audience, the king explains what the wish can really do, lays out the troubles facing the kingdom and the world, and offers a head start on any of hooks 1–7. The wish is the delivery mechanism, not a separate hook.
- **Elven path:** a paltry reward, then turned loose. Locals, refugees and character backstory drive hooks 8–10.
- **Shared arcs, two entry points:** the Maw arc (2 and 8) and the Umbra arc (3 and 10) are built once. Only the patron, contacts and starting position change, so the first sessions of each need writing for both entries.
- **Cut:** Under Tinksmith. The king would never pitch it; it can return later as a rumor.

## Hook 1: The Third Continent: the second wave
- **Head start:** places on the next fleet. Two Mayflower-like ships of about 300 passengers each founded the settlement; after losses it holds about 500. This wave, several ships, aims for 1,000. The fifth Royal Guard member leads the settlement.
- **What's there:** the Wuson (cold at first, slowly won over), the Torgen surviving in Arcanostro, the Golden Dominion stripping the ruins, Rar'than lizardfolk testing outsiders' strength, hostile Nash, the Arachnids to the north.
- **Ways to play it:**
  - Settlers and builders (fits trade professions returning around session 10).
  - Diplomats with the Wuson.
  - Salvagers in Arcanostro (reuses the crawl system).
  - **Evil:** sell Torgen tech to the Dominion, or break from the crown and stake their own claim.
- **Long-term seeds:** the truth of Arcanostro's fall (left for players to piece together), the dragon king under Rar'than and the ruins, the Mja and Mjannen, the Dominion rising late in the Uprising.

## Hook 2: Hunting the Maw
- **Head start:** contacts in the west loosely tied to the Watchers. The king doesn't know what they are.
- **What's there:** a Prophet growing more paranoid, the Great Flame / old Guiding Flame split, Red Tusk generals, and an Inquisition that hunts the Watchers. Because the party's contacts are Watcher-adjacent, Inquisitors may come to suspect the party.
- **Branches:**
  1. **Fight the Maw,** with old-faith and Red Tusk allies.
  2. **Join the Maw (evil):** further the cult's goals.
  3. **Join, then overthrow:** the old-faith faction inside the cult *(its leader yours to name)*. It exists in canon: old-faith converts who joined because they wanted the Prophet gone so people would return to the old faith, and who are fed up with how far the leader has gone.
- **Timeline lever:** in canon the Expanse drifts back to the Guiding Flame only after 1688. If branch 3 succeeds, that return could come about 40 years early, turning the 1648 assassination from the spark of a war into the start of a reformation.
- **Long-term seeds:** the leader believes he can control his Omens, and if he tries they will end him (a built-in fall); the Prophet's soul chain; the Guiding Flame's tomb.
- **Crawl tie-ins:** the old-faith priest, the Fire Walker refugees from Mob justice, the coerced cultists, the deserter.

## Hook 3: The source of the rite
- **Head start:** the king's contacts in the Dusken government and an order to investigate.
- **Step 1, always: uncover the truth.** The defector general and his rite; Umbra's research into passing the curse to others; Umbra's suspicion that, with one general defected, more are plotting against him. As a slow reveal: the head of the Royal Guard was one of the defector's circle's test subjects.
- **Step 2, two Far Cry-style campaigns** over the same ground with opposite themes:
  - **Back Umbra:** free regions held by corrupt generals *(each a regional boss, yours to name)*.
  - **Join the generals:** hunt down Umbra's loyal lieutenants.
- **The timeline:** the generals' plot runs over nine years. Outside help speeds it up: loose Watcher backing from the king's side, or outsiders shaking the foundations from hook 10. Either way the Dusken front of the Uprising changes: Umbra purges the generals early, or they take over years ahead of canon.
- **Long-term seeds:** the head of the Royal Guard's spirit gem; the Umbra cult's false claim that Umbra took Paan.

## Hook 4: The cult of vanity
- **Head start:** a quiet order to investigate Elven officials. It must be covert, since the Elves have cut ties with the Humans.
- **What's there:** a monarchy posing as a republic, the royal family tied to the Guild, the Paan church being taken apart. Mostly not evil, but the corruption is layered:
  - petty graft
  - the Guild, the crown's own pawn, which puts the king in an awkward spot
  - whoever tipped off the cult or waved its "caravans" into Edretnor
  - threads that reach Umbra or the Maw
- **Ways to play it:** expose it (which could crack the false republic early; canon has the Elves later preferring Royal Guard rule to it), or, **evil**, use what they find to blackmail officials and climb inside the system.
- **Seed:** the Sympathizer's letter is the first piece.

## Hook 5: The canal and the blockade
- **Head start:** a ship, or places on one sent to break the blockade.
- **What's there:** the Fire Walkers hold the canal; Red Tusk privateers enforce the blockade on Goblin-built warships, including about six man-o'-wars; Humans and Red Tusks clash at sea constantly.
- **Ways to play it:**
  - Run the blockade as crown privateers.
  - **Evil:** turn pirate and raid anyone.
  - Defect to the Red Tusks, who follow the old faith and resent the Great Flame (links to hook 2's faction inside the cult).
- **Long-term seeds:** the Red Tusks rule the seas after 1648; some of their generals quietly answer to the Watchers.

## Hook 6: The Dragon Wastes
- **Head start:** an introduction to the Drakenblood, help forming an expedition team, basic supplies. Players with Dwarven, southern Fire Walker, Drakenblood or Goblin backgrounds may already know the legends (worth a line in those Backgrounds when the ABCs are written).
- **What's there:** Old Draken Hold behind its weakening barrier with sealed dragon spirits and artifacts; Wastelings who worship a second-generation spirit and wear the barrier down; rift shards left by dead spirits.
- **A ready-made clock:** about a year of exposure turns a person into a Wasteling, so a crawl of the Wastes can track exposure instead of city phases.
- **Ways to play it:** classic exploration and salvage; stop the Wastelings; **evil:** help break the barrier, or sell what they find.
- **Long-term seed:** canon has the barrier breaking during the Uprising; that mini-campaign's resolution is unwritten.

## Hook 7: The northern continent
- **Head start:** a place on a third ship heading north, to a continent found by accident. The king actively wants brave adventurers for it.
- **The land:** cold but livable, and the Simulacra's homeland. The Simulacra hunt and gather the land to near-extinction, then die back while it regrows. The party arrives at the lowest point: the land is ravaged and starting to recover, and Simulacra are scarce.
- **The Simulacra:**
  - Not from Uropia. Their home planet's people were highly sapient and ruined it, then fired biological seed pods (thick sealed casings, decades of hibernation) at other planets to find food and resources. To survive deep space without ships they reduced their own brain capacity, so they arrived at low band 2: poor with tools, closer to fantasy horrors. They were the first sapient people on Uropia.
  - Solitary and territorial, and extremely adaptive: each grows into whatever its life demands, so they look like a wyvern, a griffin or a hydra.
  - A small intelligent minority exists. Some have integrated into societies on the Twin Continents or the Third Continent; some remain in the north trying to break the cycle, struggling against their own nature to consume and adapt. They know only that they came from the stars, through oral-tradition stories.
  - Generally low-tech, but they adapt to advanced technology, magitech and Mental systems.
  - Their home planet culled every Omen and Herald, and Simulacra rivaled even Agents. The forces of the universe fear them: Omens hold a grudge against Simulacra and cannot be bound to them except with extreme difficulty. Even an Adaptation Omen sees one as a competitor.
- **The reveal:** the party first fights what look like different fantasy monsters, then works out they are all one kind of creature. Suits a Monster Hunter structure.
- **Ways to play it:**
  - Hunters chasing trophies and materials (ties into gathering once trades return).
  - Researchers.
  - Diplomats: find the cycle-breakers and learn the Simulacra's history, which they barely know themselves.
  - **Exploitative:** settlers who strip the regrowth and push the next collapse along.
- **Long-term seeds:** the cycle-breakers and their struggle; the home planet and the pod-seeding (a thread that could reach the Space Era); the cycle swinging back up, which the party's presence can break or worsen.

## Hook 8: Edretnor's aftermath
- **Delivered by:** locals and refugees, after a paltry reward.
- **Early to mid game:** the refugee camps; the Death Herald driving off anyone trying to rebuild; salvage runs back into the ruins (reusing the crawl with Death Omens); prejudice against the Fire Walker refugees.
- **Late game:** becomes the Maw arc (hook 2) from the Elven side. The party's Elven patrons may themselves be part of hook 4's corruption.
- **If they killed the Cell Master:** the city survives with no Death Herald. The hook becomes rebuilding a living city and facing the Maw's retaliation for its first defeat.

## Hook 9: The Orc border
- **Delivered by:** the skirmishes near the peninsula and the smuggling through the canal.
- **Ways to play it:** fight for the Elves, or join the Orcs and Fire Walkers (serving the Prophet's regime or attaching themselves to the Red Tusk generals). Either way, wartime or the build-up to it.
- **Long-term seeds:** the ruins of the 1627 burning; Blackwell's past and the old soldier's; the 1648 junta.

## Hook 10: The shadow over the city
- **Delivered by:** Dusken or Elven PCs, or locals, who link the artificial night to the Dusken. That's right for the wrong reason: the Abyss is the Maw's rite, but the technique came from Umbra's defector.
- **Where it leads:** the Dusklands as outsiders. Same arc as hook 3 (truth first, then the two Far Cry sides), with no king's contacts. Being outsiders is what speeds up the timeline.
